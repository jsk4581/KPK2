# Usage scenario — "They pushed me out. Now my dream starts." (draft v2, 2026-10-24)

Owner: Gonzalo (GTM). Requested by Roy. Same persona as `docs/gtm/gonzalo/journey-writer.md`, new angle.
Brand: **turn the AI crisis into an opportunity.** AI, the source of the fear, becomes the ally.
Labels: everything about Maya is `simulation` (fictional). Market facts: see `docs/gtm/gonzalo/statistics.md`.

v2 change: in v1 Maya's "dream" was selling story packages, which is copywriting again, the work ChatGPT just took. Now her dream is something AI can't do: reopening her parents' bakery. AI does everything around it.

## The idea in one line

When AI pushes you out of your job, you don't have to wait for "someday" (*The Monk and the Riddle*: no deferred life plan). The agent turns your dream into a concrete, reality-checked plan, starting with almost no money. **AI can't bake Maya's bread. It can do everything else.** With AI as an ally, anyone can do almost anything, even far from their old job. And no big investment is needed: AI does the work that used to need a business plan writer, a web designer and a marketing agency, so Maya can start small (home kitchen, then a shared kitchen, delivery) instead of opening a full bakery.

## Persona

Maya R., 38, Austin, Texas. Freelance copywriter for 8 years (B2B SaaS). She was good at it, but it was never her passion. Needs at least USD 4,000/month. 10–12 free hours a week, one child. Her parents ran a family bakery for 25 years. It closed in 2019, and she still has her mother's recipe notebook. (simulation)

**Her real problem is fear, not skills.** Every day she spends about 2 hours on Upwork and LinkedIn looking for copywriting gigs, sends proposals, gets almost no replies, and goes to bed worried. The bakery dream feels too big and too risky to start. (simulation)

## Step 0 — The push

Her biggest client emails: "We now write with AI. We only need light editing: USD 800/month instead of USD 3,300." She loses 40% of her income in one email.
That night she bakes her mother's bread for the first time in years and thinks: *"Maybe this is the push."*

## Step 1 — Dream dump (free, 10 minutes)

Maya writes (or speaks) freely:
> "I don't want to write for software companies anymore. What I really miss is my parents' bakery: the smell on Saturday mornings, neighbors who came for bread and stayed to talk. I'd love to bring it back, but I know nothing about running a food business, and I can't lose more money."

She connects her context, each source opt-in, one by one:

| Connector | What the agent learns |
|---|---|
| Photos | Many bread photos, plus photos of the old bakery and the recipe notebook |
| Saved links | Sourdough videos, home-bakery Instagram accounts |
| Notes app | "Bakery?" appears 4 times since 2022 |
| LinkedIn / resume | 8 years of writing and marketing: she can tell a story and sell it |

Agent's reply: *"This isn't a new dream. It has shown up in your notes 4 times since 2022. You don't need to know business yet. That part I can help with."*

## Step 2 — Organized questions (15 minutes)

The agent doesn't give advice yet. It asks, one at a time:

1. **Dream:** What does a good Saturday look like in 2 years?
2. **Values:** Who do you want to do good for, and why? (Maya: "My neighborhood. A place where people meet, like my parents' bakery.")
3. **Life:** How many hours a week, how much money, how long can you go without full income?
4. **Assets:** What do you already have: recipes, an oven, skills, contacts, savings?
5. **Fear:** What would make you quit? (Maya: "Losing our savings, and failing in front of my family.")

Maya's answers become a one-page **Dream Brief**: dream, values, constraints, assets, fears.

## Step 3 — Three concrete versions of the dream

| Option | What it is | Fits her life? |
|---|---|---|
| A. Lean bakery, no storefront | Online pre-orders and home delivery. Bakes at home first, then rents a shared commercial kitchen by the hour when orders grow | Yes: almost no investment, keeps her weekdays free |
| B. Farmers market stand | Sells every Saturday at the market | Needs a stall fee, a tent and early mornings. Good add-on later |
| C. Rent a storefront now | Reopen the bakery as a real shop | Lease, equipment and staff before she has one customer. Too much risk today |

## Step 4 — Reality check (MBA logic)

Nobody knows what will work tomorrow, so the agent doesn't promise. It checks the numbers and proposes the smallest test. For option A:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is it legal at home? | Yes. The Texas cottage food law lets her sell homemade bread directly to customers, with no city permit, up to USD 150K a year. She needs a food handler card and labels. Pickup or in-person delivery only, no mail. (verified, see Sources) |
| Who pays? | Neighbors and families within about 5 km (simulation) |
| What does she sell? | A "Saturday box": 1 sourdough loaf + 4 pastries from her mother's recipes, USD 35 (simulation) |
| Margin | Ingredients and box about USD 12, so USD 23 per box (simulation) |
| How many to replace the lost USD 2,500/month? | 2,500 / 23 = 109 boxes a month, about 27 per weekend (simulation) |
| First goal | USD 1,000/month = 44 boxes a month, about 11 per weekend. Fits one home oven and about 10 hours (simulation) |
| Cost to start | Under USD 200: test batches, boxes, labels, food handler card, a free pre-order page (simulation) |
| Biggest risk | No customers yet, and it's tiring to bake while still freelancing |
| Verdict | **Yellow → green** if 15 neighbors pre-order in week 3 |

The reality check is honest: B gets "add-on once she has 30 regular customers," and C gets "red for now."

### The business plan AI writes: grow step by step, no big investment

| Stage | Where she bakes | How she sells | Monthly fixed cost | Move to next stage when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Test (month 1) | Home kitchen (legal under Texas cottage food law) | Pre-order site, pickup and her own delivery | About USD 0 | Her home oven is full: about 11+ boxes a weekend |
| 2. Grow (months 2–3) | Shared commercial kitchen, night slot | Pre-orders + home delivery in her area | About USD 400 for 20 hours (one Austin listing, single-source) | Steady 25+ boxes a weekend, or a café wants her bread |
| 3. Expand (month 3+) | Shared kitchen | Delivery + cafés (needs state registration) + farmers market | Same + market fee | Data shows a storefront would pay off. Maybe never needed |

Why the shared kitchen waits for stage 2: USD 400/month needs about 18 extra boxes a month just to pay for itself (400 / 23, simulation). The agent shows this math so Maya decides with numbers, not fear.

## Step 5 — Plan with a first step today

| Week | Milestone | First step |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Legal and ready | Today: take the online food handler course. The agent writes her label text |
| 2 | Test bake | Bake her mother's 3 best recipes and price each one with the agent |
| 3 | First pre-orders | Pre-order page plus the story of her parents' bakery. Goal: 15 boxes |
| 4 | First Saturday | Bake and hand out the boxes. Goal: sold out |

She keeps her 2 remaining copywriting clients as a bridge. She is not asked to quit anything.

**What AI does for her, free** (her ally, not her competitor):

| AI does | Maya does |
|---|---|
| Business plan: costs, price, break-even, the 3 stages above | Bakes |
| Website: builds her pre-order and delivery site | Talks to neighbors and customers |
| Marketing strategy: neighborhood launch, tasting day, Nextdoor and Instagram posts, the story of her parents' bakery | Chooses the recipes and the feel |
| Rules: what Texas allows, label text, when she needs a registration | Decides how much she wants to grow |
| Operations: shopping list, baking schedule, delivery route, shared-kitchen booking based on the orders | Approves the plan each week |

## Step 5b — Daily nudges: from fear to one small step a day

A plan alone doesn't beat fear. Every day the agent sends Maya one small nudge: a step that takes 15–60 minutes and is too small to be scary. It celebrates each one. (all simulation)

| Day | Nudge | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | "Good morning. Today's step: 20 minutes to finish the food handler course. That's all." | Tiny, clear, done today |
| Tue | "Pick the one recipe from your mother's notebook you'd bake for a stranger." | Emotional, not business |
| Wed | "Your label text is ready. Read it and change one word if you want." | AI did the hard part, she only approves |
| Thu | "Your copywriting search is done for today: I checked Upwork and LinkedIn, 1 match, I saved it for you. Now 30 minutes for the bakery." | Takes the job-search worry off her plate |
| Fri | "Bake tonight. Send me a photo when it's out of the oven." | Action, and progress collected by photo |
| Sat | "Week 1 done: course ✓, recipe ✓, labels ✓, first bake ✓. Your mom's bread is coming back." | Celebrates progress, builds the habit |

**Her time shifts from worry to the dream:**

| | Before (week 0) | After (week 4) |
|---|---|---|
| Copywriting job search | About 2 hours a day, anxious | About 15 minutes a day: the agent watches for gigs and only shows real matches |
| Copywriting work | 2 remaining clients | Same 2 clients, as a bridge. The job didn't die, she just stopped chasing it every day |
| Bakery | 0 hours, "someday" | About 1 hour a day + Friday night baking |
| How she feels | Worried every night | "It's starting to take shape." |

## Step 6 — Progress, the day off, and the "vaccine"

- **Progress is collected, not reported.** Connectors see new photos of test bakes and the pre-order count, so Maya doesn't fill in forms.
- **Day off:** in week 2 she skips the test bake for 3 days (her son is sick). The agent says: *"You didn't fail. You took a pause. Let's test 1 recipe this week instead of 3."*
- **Barrier detected:** her Instagram post gets 40 likes but only 3 pre-orders. The agent recognizes a pattern seen in many similar plans (aggregated and anonymized): a home bakery's first customers come from tasting, not from social media. This is her **vaccine**, a fix for a known barrier before it makes her quit.
  *"Try this: bake 20 mini loaves and give them to your neighbors on Sunday, with a card and a QR code for pre-orders."*

## Step 7 — Week 4

The neighbors taste the bread. Pre-orders reach 18 boxes, and her first Saturday sells out: USD 630 in sales (simulation). **First income from her dream.** A café down the street asks to sell her bread. The agent flags that this needs a separate state registration and more oven space. It updates the business plan: book a shared-kitchen night slot in month 2, register for café sales in month 3. Next goal: 25 boxes a weekend, with delivery. Total invested so far: under USD 200 (simulation).

That night Maya realizes she hasn't opened Upwork in 3 days, and she didn't worry about it. Her copywriting clients are still there as a bridge, but her time and energy now go to the dream, which is slowly taking shape.

## Behind the scenes: why it gets better with every user

1. Each person adds context (dream, constraints, steps, results).
2. Plans that worked and barriers that stopped people are learned **in aggregate and anonymized**, never person by person.
3. Each new user gets a sharper reality check and better "vaccines" for barriers.
4. The more people use it, the stronger the knowledge base (our MCP) becomes. That's the moat.
